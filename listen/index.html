The plates

The archive, drawer by drawer.

Plate I

The masking booth

Try it

Hand the clerk something with a name on it, and watch the name leave the building.

How it is read

Patterns catch email addresses, phone numbers and @handles and replace them. The author is then swapped for a one-way code, so the same person keeps the same code without anyone being able to read a name back out of it. Everything happens on your own device.

Plate II

Anatomy of a record

One post, opened on the desk. The little numerals show which chapters read each field.

How it is read

A real row from the dataset. Bio and location are read by the software but sealed here, because no chapter ever shows them to a visitor.

Plate III

The ledger

By door

Who came through which door, and how often they brought company.

How it is read

Posts, unique anonymous voices, posts per voice, and the share of posts that react to an earlier post.

Plate IV

Ink on the page

Completeness

No archive is perfect. Here is exactly how many blanks this one holds.

How it is read

Share of rows with a usable value in each field. Reference is only expected on posts that react to something, so it is measured against those.

Plate V

The wall of hours

Day by hour, UTC

Days across, hours down, one lit window for each. Click a window to read who was up.

How it is read

Brighter means more posts arrived in that hour. Times are UTC, so a lit band at night here is somebody’s lunchtime elsewhere.

Plate VI

Manners at the door

Kinds of post

Some arrive with something to say. Others arrive holding somebody else’s.

How it is read

Original posts, replies, quotes, reposts and cross-platform shares as a share of each door’s traffic.

Plate VII

The pile grows

Cumulative

The whole run of filing, door by door.

How it is read

Running total of posts per platform across the month.

Plate VIII

A thread, unspooled

Chronology

Pull one conversation out of the pile and lay it along the clock.

How it is read

Left to right is time, stretched at the start where most reactions happen. Height is how many hops a post is from the original. Each line ties a post to the one it reacted to.

Rules of the post

Four checkpoints, and a few things the clerk is forbidden to keep.

1

Hear

Each post is taken in from its door and tagged with where it came from.

2

Stamp

One clock for everybody. Every time is rewritten in UTC so the month reads in order.

3

Mask

Authors become codes. Emails, numbers and handles are struck out of the text.

4

Thread

Every reply, quote, repost and share is tied to the post that set it off.
