Plate I

The barometer

Try it

How it is read

A small wordlist with negation and emphasis, run on your device. It is here to show how the reading works, and it will happily miss a joke. English only.

Plate II

Seven kinds of air

Feelings

Mood says warm or cold. Feeling says what is actually blowing in. Tap a petal to chart it.

How it is read

Wording rules sort each post into one feeling, across all the languages in the flock. Sarcasm is the hardest and the least reliable.

Plate III

Climate zones

Feeling by group

Everyone is sure their own town has the worst weather.

How it is read

Each number is the share of that group’s posts carrying that feeling. Groups under 5 people are left out.

Plate IV

The river of feeling

All feelings

The month’s mood as one long current, thick where the crowd was loud.

How it is read

Each band is one feeling, stacked by day. Tap a petal in Seven kinds of air to lift a single feeling out of the stream.

Plate V

Fronts on the map

Trends

Every trend carries its own weather. A few of them change their mind halfway.

How it is read

Each line is a trend’s mood over its active days, above the dotted line warm, below it sour. The verdict compares its first half with its second.

Plate VI

Around the clock

By hour, UTC

Is the crowd kinder in the morning? Consult the bars.

How it is read

Warm minus sour as a share of posts in each UTC hour, from all time zones at once.

Plate VII

Further down the thread

Depth

Conversations have altitude. Watch the temperature as they climb.

How it is read

Mood of posts by how many hops they sit from the original post. Dots shrink when few posts reach that depth.

How the forecast is made

Four instruments, and a few things no forecaster should promise.

1

Read

Each post arrives with a mood: warm, even or sour.

2

Sort

Wording rules name the feeling, from excitement to sarcasm.

3

Average

Days, trends and groups are rolled up. Warm minus sour gives the temperature.

4

Announce

The numbers become weather, because nobody ever argued with a cloud.
